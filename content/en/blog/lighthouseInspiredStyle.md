As you may know, I'm a big fan of horror movies, and recently I watched  [The Lighthouse](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt7984734/?ref_=nv_sr_srsg_0_tt_8_nm_0_in_0_q_The%2520Lig) (2019) by Robert Eggers. I loved it and highly recommend it.
<br>
It was the cinematography that impressed and inspired me most. Every shot is a work of art in itself and a complete visual feast. The film is in black and white and in a <b>1.19:1</b> aspect ratio, which is rather unconventional for a modern film. 
<br>
Furthermore, the film is shot on orthochromatic film, which is a type of black and white film sensitive to blue and green light but not to red light, which makes the red hues appear very black ([Source](https://theasc.com/articles/stormy-isle-the-lighthouse)).

Furthermore, in the [PiXimpefect](https://www.youtube.com/c/PiXimperfect) tutorials on YouTube, Unmesh often uses a similar technique to easily see skin imperfections for faster corrections. Essentially, he applies a black and white filter to the photo and pulls the red tones towards black, which highlights the skin flaws.
<br>
<br>
Basically, all of this inspired me to adopt a creative style in some of my photos. Of course, this style is definitely not flattering to the subject, but I still think it gives an interesting look to a photo that would otherwise be rather ordinary.
<br>
Before showing you the photos, here are the editing steps to achieve a style inspired by The Lighthouse in Adobe Lightroom:

1. **Apply a black and white profile**
2. **In the black and white mixer, blend the red completely to black.**
3. **For more dirt and imperfections, blend the orange slightly to black.**
4. **Adjust Texture and Clarity to taste.**
5. **Adjust curves and color grading to taste.**
6. **Never show the photo to the model to avoid creating insecurities.**

Here are some of my photos in this style that I really like. Furthermore, I used my [Sony FE 50mm F1.8](https://www.sony.ca/en/electronics/camera-lenses/sel50f18f) lens, which does not help at all to beautify the subject.
